4 Abstract
5 Reliability-based design of high waste dumps needs the drained shear strength of rockfill and 
6 mine waste rock as a distribution, together with its dependence on stress and a rule to combine 
7 published experience with site tests. Leps-P is a three-level hierarchical model of the secant 
8 friction angle in which the dilatant part of strength decays with stress toward an asymptote, so 
9 that the curvature of the envelope decreases as dilatancy is suppressed. It was calibrated on 767 
10 large-scale triaxial tests from 36 publications in the public Leps 2.0 database and evaluated by 
11 leaving out one publication at a time. The data supported a curvature that decays with stress 
12 (likelihood ratio test, p < 0.001) but did not identify the asymptotic angle, which was therefore 
13 averaged over the likelihood ridge. Out of sample, the bounded form scored better than a 
14 quadratic log–log form (continuous ranked probability score 0.0670 versus 0.0688, Wilcoxon p = 
15 0.04) and removed the low bias of the median above 2.75 MPa. With a few site tests at low 
16 stress, a prespecified level-only update predicted the strength at higher stress better than a least￾17 squares fit with a fixed curvature (score 0.0278 versus 0.0369). At field scale, the uncertainty of 
18 an untested waste rock came mainly from specimen size and from material and laboratory 
19 variability; the unidentified asymptote contributed about 4% of the variance at 10 MPa. Within￾20 material comparisons did not detect a systematic specimen-size effect. For a 700 m dump of 
21 waste rock from an open-pit mine, placement density and untested rock types controlled the 
22 margin of safety. The model, design charts, and code allow re-estimation as tests at higher stress 
23 become available.

24 Practical Applications
25 Designers of high waste dumps and rockfill dams often have few or no large-scale shear tests of 
26 their own and rely on published charts of rockfill strength. This work turns the most widely used 
27 chart into a tool that gives a best estimate of strength together with a cautious value for design, 
28 for any rock quality and density. It shows that the strength of crushed rock flattens at high 
29 pressure in the way expected from the loss of dilation, and that this flattening can be used safely 
30 within the range of available tests. A few laboratory tests of the site material at low pressure 
31 sharpen the estimate at higher pressure, and the tool warns the user when site tests disagree with 
32 published experience. Most of the remaining uncertainty for an untested material comes from the 
33 size of the rock particles and from differences between materials and laboratories, so testing the 
34 actual waste rock at large scale is the most valuable investigation. For a very high dump, the 
35 loose state of placed waste rock and rock types without tests governed the safety margin. Free 
36 software accompanies the paper.

39 Introduction
40 Leps (1970) compiled 109 large-scale triaxial tests and proposed three nonlinear 
41 envelopes for rockfill that have been used for dams and waste dumps whenever site-specific tests 
42 were unavailable. Cylwik et al. (2025) updated them with a public database of 938 tests (Arrieta 
43 2025), adopted the power criterion of De Mello (1977) and Charles and Watts (1980) with a 
single fitting parameter, the secant friction angle at 𝜎𝑛
′ 44 = 10𝑝𝑎, and related that angle to rock 
45 quality and void ratio. That recent and widely adopted update is referred to as Leps 2.0.

--- page 3 ---
3
46 Two developments motivate a further extension. First, waste dumps now reach 500–700 
47 m (Valenzuela et al. 2008), so effective normal stresses on deep failure surfaces approach or 
48 exceed the 7.9 MPa reached in the database, in a material that crushes and loses dilatancy 
49 (Marsal 1973; Hardin 1985; Lade et al. 1996; Linero et al. 2007; Ovalle et al. 2020). How the 
50 envelope behaves beyond the data then matters, and a power law with constant exponent keeps 
51 curving downward indefinitely, whereas the strength of a granular material is bounded below by 
52 its critical state (Bolton 1986; Jefferies and Been 2016). Second, design is moving toward 
53 reliability-based criteria (Read and Stacey 2009; Hawley and Cunning 2017), which require a 
54 strength distribution and a rule to combine published experience with site tests.
55 Hierarchical models that borrow strength from generic databases have been developed for 
56 soil properties (Ching and Phoon 2019; Ching et al. 2021), and a hierarchical Bayesian analysis 
57 of the Barton and Kjærnsli (1981) parameters has been applied to rockfill groups of a single 
58 project (Contreras et al. 2022; Contreras and Linero-Molina 2025). The contributions of this 
59 work are (1) an envelope whose curvature decays with stress as dilatancy is suppressed, tested 
60 against a fixed-form alternative out of sample and within materials; (2) the separation of 
61 publication and material variability; (3) a prespecified site-updating rule with a prior–data 
62 conflict check, compared with the simple fits used in practice; (4) a decomposition of the 
63 uncertainty of an untested waste rock at field scale; and (5) design charts and an open 
64 implementation. Leps-P is an independent extension built on the public Leps 2.0 database; it is 
65 not affiliated with the authors of Leps 2.0.

148 effects are thus estimated conditionally (an empirical Bayes scheme). Convergence was checked

224 Sources of Uncertainty at Field Scale
225 For an untested material of low rock quality placed at e = 0.65 and scaled from 0.18 m to 
226 1 m particles, the standard deviation of the mean-envelope ln τ at 3 MPa corresponds to about 
3.5° in 𝜙𝑠
′ 227 . Specimen size contributed 47%, between-set variability 30%, publication 19%, the 
228 unidentified asymptote 1%, and the fixed effects 2% (Fig. 5). At 10 MPa, beyond the data, the 
229 asymptote contributed 4%. The extrapolation of the envelope form is therefore a smaller source 
230 of uncertainty than the identity of the material and the scale of the particles.
231 Application: Waste Rock from a Large Open-Pit Mine
232 Site Data
233 The waste rock of a large open-pit mine hosted in carbonate rocks comprises five rock 
234 types, A to E, of which A is a metacarbonate, B a silicate metasedimentary rock, C an igneous 
235 rock, and D and E carbonate and argillaceous sedimentary rocks. Drained triaxial tests on large 
236 specimens of scalped gradation by one laboratory were available for A, B, and C (5 sets). 
237 Illustrative mass fractions of 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, and 10% were used. Rock quality was taken 
238 as low for A, C, D, and E and moderate for B. For rock type A, the conflict check gave p = 0.16 
239 with the low class, 0.11 with the moderate class, and 0.025 with the good class, so the tests were 
240 consistent with the class assigned.
241 Conflict Check and Failure Criterion
242 The conflict check flagged 3 of the 5 sets (Fig. 6a): both sets of rock type B (p ≤ 0.0006) 
243 and one set of rock type C (p = 0.042). In both B sets the secant angle increased with stress and 
244 the specimens were still contracting at the maximum deviator, reached at 13–19% axial strain. 

--- page 12 ---
12
245 Because the full stress–strain curves were tabulated in the test reports, the sets were re-reduced 
246 with the deviator at 10% and 15% axial strain: the free log–log slope of the B sets remained 
247 above one (1.04–1.19), so the increase is not an artifact of the failure definition. Fixed-strain 
248 criteria created new conflicts in the other rock types, which confirms that site tests must be 
249 reduced with the criterion of the calibration database. Flagged sets did not update the envelope; 
250 because they are stronger than the prior at high stress, excluding them is conservative here, 
251 although not as a general rule. The remaining sets were updated jointly with a common 
252 laboratory effect.

256 by an additional increase of 0.15. A failure surface through a mixed dump mobilizes the mass￾257 weighted strength of the rock types it crosses; the envelopes were sampled jointly, sharing 
hyperparameters, fixed effects, laboratory effect, and size shift. At 𝜎𝑛
′ 258 = 3 MPa the median and 
5th percentile of the dump-average 𝜙𝑠
′ 259 were 37.3° and 35.6° at laboratory density and size, and

274 correlation assumption, each sample was evaluated on a family of circles and, when the factor of 
275 safety was below 1.3, by a full search of the critical surface. The model correlation gave 0 
276 failures (probability of failure 0.0–0.6%, Clopper–Pearson 95% interval; Clopper and Pearson 
277 1934), and a surface confined to the untested, degraded rock type E gave 11 (1.8%, 0.9–3.3%). 
278 These are conditional probabilities that reflect strength uncertainty only, for a drained foundation 
279 and a two-dimensional section. The factor of safety computed with the 5th-percentile envelope 
280 was close to the 5th percentile of the factor of safety (1.25 versus 1.26), because shared level 
281 shifts dominate the uncertainty here; this need not hold when pore pressure, curvature 
282 uncertainty, or independent materials dominate.
283 Discussion
284 What Leps-P Adds
285 Leps-P does not predict the central strength of an untested material better than Leps 2.0. 
286 Its contributions are an envelope whose curvature decays with stress, which removes the low 
287 bias of fixed-form fits at high stress and is supported within materials; an uncertainty that is 
288 calibrated at low stress, where the constant-dispersion reference is not, and wide but unbiased at 
289 high stress; a prespecified updating rule that outperformed the simple fits used in practice; and a 
290 decomposition showing that, for an untested waste rock, the material and the particle scale matter 

--- page 14 ---
14
291 more than the extrapolation of the envelope form. The last result indicates where investigation is 
292 most valuable: large-scale tests of the actual waste rock remove most of the material and 
293 laboratory components, and tests of one material at two particle sizes would constrain the largest 
294 remaining term.
295 Design Format
296 A design procedure consistent with these results is: (1) assign rock quality and placement 
297 void ratio to each rock type and check the conflict diagnostic for any site tests, reduced with the 
298 maximum-deviator criterion; (2) update with the tests that pass, at the level only (Eq. 7); (3) shift 
299 the envelopes to placement density and field particle size; (4) adopt as characteristic value for a 
300 global mechanism the 5th percentile of the mean envelope, a Bayesian cautious estimate of the 
301 mean in the sense of Eurocode 7 (CEN 2004, 2024; Prästings et al. 2019); (5) check the 
302 probability of failure by sampling the envelopes with stated correlations between rock types, 
303 including the weakest-rock-type scenario; and (6) choose the target factor of safety for the 5th￾304 percentile envelope from the consequence class. Table 5 pairs both quantities for the example: a 
305 factor of safety of about 1.25 with the 5th-percentile envelope corresponded to no failures in 600 
306 samples, and about 1.09 to a conditional probability of failure near 2%. Applying factors 
307 calibrated for best-estimate parameters (Read and Stacey 2009; Hawley and Cunning 2017) to 
308 the 5th-percentile envelope would count the strength uncertainty twice.
309 Limitations
The database contains no tests above 𝜎𝑛
′ 310 = 7.9 MPa and its tests above 3 MPa come from 
311 eight publications, so the bounded form is supported within the data and only its consequences 
312 are explored beyond; the asymptotic angle is not identified. The lower tail of the population is 
truncated at 𝜙1
′
313 = 31°, class labels may carry information from measured strength, and the 

--- page 15 ---
15
314 within-material residual is laboratory scatter rather than spatial variability in the dump. The site 
315 application uses one laboratory and illustrative proportions. Pore pressure in saturated basal 
316 layers, wetting collapse (Oldecop and Alonso 2001), seismic loading, three-dimensional 
317 geometry, and static liquefaction of contractive waste are outside the scope; they require their 
318 own analyses, for which the drained envelopes and their uncertainty are an input. Leps-P can be 
re-estimated as data appear, in particular drained tests at 𝜎3
′
319 of 5–10 MPa on carbonate and 
320 argillaceous waste rock and tests of one material at two sizes with parallel gradation.
321 Conclusions
322 1. The calibration data support an envelope whose curvature decays with stress toward an 
323 asymptote (p = 8e-05), consistent with the suppression of dilatancy; the asymptotic angle is 
324 not identified and is averaged over the likelihood ridge.
325 2. Out of sample, the bounded form scored better than a quadratic log–log form and removed 
326 the low bias of the median at high stress, but it did not predict the central strength of a new 
327 material better than Leps 2.0 with constant dispersion.
328 3. With a few site tests at low stress, a prespecified level-only update predicted the strength at 
329 higher stress better than least-squares fits with a fixed curvature; its intervals were wider than 
330 nominal.
331 4. No systematic specimen-size effect was detected within materials; the heterogeneity of the 
332 size effect is the largest source of uncertainty of an untested waste rock at field scale, 
333 followed by material and laboratory variability.
334 5. For a 700 m dump on a competent foundation, placement density and untested rock types 
335 controlled the margin of safety more than the form of the envelope.
