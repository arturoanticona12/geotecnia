1 Leps-P: Probabilistic Strength Envelopes with Stress-Decaying Curvature for Rockfill and 
2 Mine Waste Rock

4 Abstract
5 Reliability-based design of high waste dumps needs the drained shear strength of rockfill and 
6 mine waste rock as a distribution, with its dependence on stress and a rule to combine published 
7 experience with site tests. Leps-P is a three-level hierarchical model of the secant friction angle 
8 in which the dilatant part of strength decays with stress toward an asymptote; each material has 
9 its own dilatant level and decay rate. It was calibrated on 767 large-scale triaxial tests from 36 
10 publications of the public Leps 2.0 database. The likelihood favored this form over a quadratic 
11 log–log envelope with the same number of parameters (log-likelihood difference 9.8) and over a 
12 form without an asymptote (44.7). The asymptote, about 25°, is a population decay parameter 
13 well below the angles measured at the highest stresses, not a critical-state angle. Leaving out one 
14 publication at a time, the bounded, quadratic, and Leps 2.0 envelopes predicted the strength of 
15 new materials with statistically indistinguishable accuracy, but only Leps-P had calibrated 
16 intervals at low stress. With a few site tests at low stress, updating the level of each material gave 
17 calibrated predictions at higher stress (83% coverage of the 80% interval); differences from 
18 simpler fits were not significant with 13 publications. At field scale, about 62% of the variance 
19 of the strength of an untested waste rock at 3 MPa came from the assumed heterogeneity of the 
20 specimen-size effect. For a 700 m dump on a competent foundation, analyzed with the 
21 Morgenstern–Price method, the factor of safety was 1.47 with the median and 1.20 with the 5th￾22 percentile envelope of placed waste rock. Design charts and open code are provided.

23 Practical Applications
24 Designers of high waste dumps and rockfill dams often have few or no large-scale shear tests and 
25 rely on published charts of rockfill strength. This work turns the Leps chart into a tool that gives 
26 a best estimate of strength and a cautious value for design, for any rock quality and density, 
27 together with the range of uncertainty. Its curves flatten at high pressure, as expected when 
28 crushed rock stops dilating, and they are supported by tests up to about 8 MPa on the failure 
29 plane. A few tests of the site material at low pressure sharpen the estimate at higher pressure, and 
30 the tool warns when site tests disagree with published experience. For untested waste rock, most 
31 of the remaining uncertainty comes, under the adopted model, from the size of the rock particles 
32 and from differences between materials and laboratories; testing the actual waste rock at large 
33 scale and at two particle sizes is therefore the most valuable investigation. Free software 
34 accompanies the paper.

37 Introduction
38 Leps (1970) compiled 109 large-scale triaxial tests and proposed three nonlinear 
39 envelopes for rockfill that have been used for dams and waste dumps whenever site-specific tests 
40 were unavailable. Cylwik et al. (2025) updated them with a public database of 938 tests (Arrieta 
41 2025), adopted the power criterion of De Mello (1977) and Charles and Watts (1980) with a 
single fitting parameter, the secant friction angle at 𝜎𝑛
′ 42 = 10𝑝𝑎, and related that angle to rock 
43 quality and void ratio. That update is referred to here as Leps 2.0.
44 Two developments motivate a further extension. First, design is moving toward 
45 reliability-based criteria (Read and Stacey 2009; Hawley and Cunning 2017), which require a 

--- page 3 ---
3
46 strength distribution and a rule to combine published experience with site tests. Second, waste 
47 dumps now reach 500–700 m (Valenzuela et al. 2008). For a uniform dump on a competent 
48 foundation the critical surface stays at normal stresses of a few megapascals, but mechanisms 
49 controlled by the foundation, by a basal layer, or by the weakest material reach higher stresses, 
50 where waste rock crushes and loses dilatancy (Marsal 1973; Hardin 1985; Lade et al. 1996; 
51 Linero et al. 2007; Ovalle et al. 2020). How an envelope behaves at the edge of and beyond the 
52 data then matters: a power law with a constant exponent keeps curving downward, whereas the 
53 excess of strength over the critical state, which is the contribution of dilatancy, is suppressed by 
54 confinement (Bolton 1986; Jefferies and Been 2016).
55 Hierarchical models that borrow strength from generic databases have been developed for 
56 soil properties (Ching and Phoon 2019; Ching et al. 2021), and a hierarchical Bayesian analysis 
57 of the Barton and Kjærnsli (1981) parameters has been applied to the rockfill groups of a single 
58 project (Contreras et al. 2022; Contreras and Linero-Molina 2025). Leps-P differs in pooling 36 
59 publications, in its envelope form, and in its out-of-sample evaluation. Its contributions are (1) an 
60 envelope whose curvature decays with stress for every material, compared with fixed-form 
61 alternatives by likelihood and out of sample; (2) the separation of publication and material 
62 variability; (3) a site-updating rule with a prior–data conflict check, compared with the simple 
63 fits used in practice; (4) a decomposition of the uncertainty of an untested waste rock at field 
64 scale; and (5) design charts and an open implementation.

209 Specimen Size
210 Sixteen materials of 12 publications were tested at maximum particle sizes differing by a 
211 factor of two or more. With set intercepts adjusted for void ratio and materials combined within 
212 publications, the random-effects mean of 𝛽𝐷 was +0.041 (Knapp–Hartung 95% CI −0.001 to 
213 +0.084; Knapp and Hartung 2003) with a between-publication standard deviation τ = 0.063 (Q￾214 profile 95% CI 0.042–0.107; Viechtbauer 2007; Fig. S4). Adding the between-set variance of the

216 about −0.05 to −0.15 implied by Weibull scaling of homothetic gradations, while the database

218 trends have been reported (Varadarajan et al. 2003). For design, Leps-P uses a zero mean with 
219 the larger τ as uncertainty.

228 Application: Waste Rock from a Large Open-Pit Mine
229 Site Data and Conflict Check
230 The waste rock of a large open-pit mine comprises five rock types, A to E. Drained 
231 triaxial tests on large specimens of scalped gradation by one laboratory were available for A, B, 
232 and C (5 sets); D and E were untested, and E is prone to degradation. Rock quality was taken as 
233 low for A, C, D, and E and moderate for B; illustrative mass fractions of 35%, 25%, 20%, 10%, 
234 and 10% were used. For rock type A the conflict check gave p = 0.18 with the low class and 
235 0.026 with the good class, so the tests were consistent with the class assigned. The check flagged 
236 3 of the 5 sets (Fig. 4a): both sets of B (p ≤ 0.0001) and one of C (p = 0.025). In the B sets the 
237 secant angle increased with stress and the specimens were still contracting at the maximum 
238 deviator, reached at 13–19% axial strain. Re-reducing them from the tabulated stress–strain 
239 curves with the deviator at 10% and 15% axial strain gave free log–log slopes of 1.04–1.19, each 
240 from three tests, so the increase is not an artifact of the failure definition, although slopes near 
241 one are not significantly above it. A slope above one is excluded at the population level by the 
242 model, not for individual materials. Fixed-strain criteria created new conflicts in the other rock 
243 types, which confirms that site tests must be reduced with the criterion of the calibration 
244 database. Flagged sets did not update the envelope, and the remaining sets were updated jointly 
245 with a common laboratory effect; each rock type is represented by one material.

272 Discussion
273 What Leps-P Adds
274 Leps-P does not predict the central strength of an untested material better than Leps 2.0, 
275 and with few site tests its advantage over simple fits is not statistically demonstrable with the 
276 available publications. Its contributions are a material-level envelope whose curvature decays 
277 with stress, strongly favored by the likelihood and consistent with the suppression of dilatancy; 
278 intervals that are calibrated where a constant dispersion is not, both without and with site tests; 
279 an updating rule with a conflict check; and a decomposition showing that, under the adopted size 
280 model, particle size and the identity of the material dominate the uncertainty of an untested waste 
281 rock. Large-scale tests of the actual waste rock, and of one material at two particle sizes, are 
282 therefore the most valuable investigations.
283 Design Format
284 A procedure consistent with these results is: (1) assign rock quality and placement void 
285 ratio to each rock type, reduce any site tests with the maximum-deviator criterion, and check the 
286 conflict diagnostic; (2) update with the tests that pass (Eq. 8); (3) shift the envelopes to 
287 placement density and field particle size; (4) adopt as characteristic value for a global mechanism 
288 the 5th percentile of the mean envelope, a Bayesian cautious estimate of the mean (Prästings et 
289 al. 2019) in the spirit of Eurocode 7 (CEN 2004, 2024); and (5) check the probability of failure 
290 by sampling the envelopes with stated correlations between rock types, including the weakest￾291 rock-type case. Because the factor of safety in limit equilibrium divides the shear strength, the 

--- page 14 ---
14
292 factor computed with the characteristic envelope equals the strength factor that the design would 
293 still tolerate; applying mining acceptance criteria calibrated for best-estimate strength (Read and 
294 Stacey 2009; Hawley and Cunning 2017) on top of it would partly count the strength uncertainty 
295 twice, since those criteria also cover model, pore-pressure, and geometric uncertainty. No target 
296 values are proposed here; the pairing of factor of safety and conditional probability of failure in 
297 Table 5 is an example to be calibrated site by site.
298 Limitations
The validity domain is 𝜎𝑛
′ 299 ≤ 8 MPa and, above 3 MPa, e ≤ 0.55, where 83 of the 90 high￾300 stress tests lie; percentiles beyond it are model extrapolations. Tests above 3 MPa come from 
eight publications, the lower tail of the population is truncated at 𝜙1
′
301 = 31°, class labels may carry 
302 information from measured strength, and the within-material residual is laboratory scatter rather 
303 than spatial variability. The size-effect heterogeneity, the largest term at field scale, rests on 12 
304 publications. The site application uses one laboratory and illustrative proportions. Pore pressure 
305 in saturated basal layers, wetting collapse (Oldecop and Alonso 2001), seismic loading, three￾306 dimensional geometry, and static liquefaction are outside the scope. Leps-P can be re-estimated 
as drained tests at 𝜎3
′
307 of 5–10 MPa, tests of one material at two sizes, and in situ density data 
308 become available.
309 Conclusions
1. An envelope in which the dilatant part of tan 𝜙𝑠
′ 310 decays with stress toward an asymptote, 
311 with a material-level dilatant level and decay rate, was strongly favored by the likelihood 
312 over fixed-form alternatives; the asymptote (about 25°) is a decay parameter, not a critical￾313 state angle.

--- page 15 ---
15
314 2. Out of sample, the bounded, quadratic, and Leps 2.0 envelopes predicted new materials with 
315 indistinguishable accuracy; Leps-P intervals were calibrated at low stress, where a constant 
316 dispersion was not.
317 3. Updating the level of each material with a few site tests at low stress gave calibrated 
318 predictions at higher stress; its advantage over simpler fits was not statistically significant 
319 with 13 publications.
320 4. No systematic specimen-size effect was detected within materials; under the adopted model 
321 its heterogeneity dominates the uncertainty of an untested waste rock at field scale.
322 5. For a 700 m dump on a competent foundation, analyzed with the Morgenstern–Price method, 
323 placement density lowered the median strength and size uncertainty the cautious strength; the 
324 envelope form did not affect the factor of safety.
